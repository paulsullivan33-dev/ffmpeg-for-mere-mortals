# Writing Criteria

Every guide in this repo is written to the same
standard. If you're adding or editing a guide —
human or AI — hold it to these.

1. **Plain words, real ideas.** Simple language,
   never dumbed-down concepts. Explain jargon on
   first use; collect terms in the glossary.
2. **No hype.** No marketing adjectives, no
   "revolutionary," no promises about what
   something will do for the reader.
3. **Honest about limits.** Say what the thing
   *can't* do. State uncertainty instead of
   smoothing it over. Validation notes include
   what remains unverified.
4. **No invented numbers.** Benchmarks, defaults,
   and specs are either measured or qualified with
   "depends on your setup." If it wasn't tested,
   say so.
5. **Checkable.** Commands are meant to be run,
   with "Check:" blocks showing what success
   looks like. Sources are linked, not asserted.
6. **Respect the reader's setup.** Call out
   Windows/Mac/Linux differences. Don't assume a
   GPU, a budget, or prior experience.
7. **Safety isn't skipped.** Real risks get
   stated plainly, not buried.
8. **Scoped.** Each guide says what it covers and
   points elsewhere for the rest. No guide
   pretends to be the whole subject.
9. **Structure over prose.** Tables, before/after
   examples, symptom → fix. Scannable beats essay.

## Verification notes for this guide

Standards 3 and 4 applied to this guide,
specifically. ffmpeg and ffprobe **8.1.2**
(the Ubuntu build, configuration line beginning
`--prefix=/usr … --enable-gpl`, with
libx264, libmp3lame, and libopus present per
the `-encoders` capture) were on the build
machine, and every quoted output is a capture
from them. Working transcripts are kept with
the build (`ffmpeg-lab/transcripts/`,
01–14, mirroring the chapters).

**The lab.** All fixtures are generated by
ffmpeg itself from lavfi test sources — no
recorded or downloaded media — by the script
shipped as `examples/make-fixtures.sh`:
`tone.wav` (sine 440 Hz, 6 s), `voice.wav`
(sine 196 Hz at volume 0.06, 8 s; its quietness
was then *measured*, not assumed: volumedetect
mean −45.5 dB / max −42.5 dB, astats peak
−42.49 dB agreeing), `song.flac` (three sine
sources mixed with `amix`, 6 s), `clip.mp4`
(`testsrc2` 320×180@24 + sine, libx264 with
`-g 48` so keyframes fall every 2 s, AAC,
12 s), `part1.mp4`/`part2.mp4` (identical
recipes, 3 s each), `scene.mp4` (`testsrc2`
640×360@30, CRF 12, 6 s). The shipped
fixtures are the build machine's own outputs
of that script.

**Capture hygiene (declared trims):**
ffmpeg's per-conversion narration is long;
chapters quote the lines a claim rests on
(input summary, stream mapping, final size
line, probe reports) and elide the rest
with `...`; full logs live in the
transcripts. Memory addresses in filter
log prefixes (`@ 0x…`) are shortened to
`0x...` where quoted. The repeated `probe()`
definition appears in transcripts exactly
as chapters print it. Dates and `ls`
columns beyond size are the capture runs'
own. One systematic difference, declared:
the lab harness ran from a `work/`
directory beside the fixtures, so
transcript commands carry `../fixtures/`
path prefixes; chapters print the same
commands in the reader's layout (bare
filenames, run from inside `examples/`),
which is also how `examples/commands.txt`
collects them. No output line was altered
by that normalization — only the paths
typed, never the results printed.

**Documented-only (not run), and why:**

- *The overwrite prompt itself.* The lab
  ran with `-y` (non-interactive harness),
  so ffmpeg's "Overwrite? [y/N]" question
  was never captured; chapter 3 describes
  it from documented behaviour and tells
  the reader to meet it deliberately on a
  disposable file.
- *Real-world footage behaviour* — phone
  video, camera files, actual music and
  speech. The fixtures are synthetic by
  design (deterministic, buildable
  anywhere); where a result depends on the
  material (VBR pricing, CRF pricing, GIF
  palette economics, fps savings), the
  chapter states the fixture result as a
  fixture result and the general behaviour
  as expectation, not measurement.
- *Named, not taught:* the concat filter
  (mismatched-piece joining), ffplay,
  streaming and device capture, hardware
  acceleration, two-pass bitrate-target
  encoding, `drawtext` and the wider
  filter graph, HandBrake and other GUI
  front ends. Each is named where a reader
  would go looking for it, with its
  boundary stated.
- *Installation* on a reader's machine is
  described, not performed — the build
  machine already had ffmpeg 8.1.2.

Specimen discipline, for the record: the
lab contains no recorded media, no real
voices or footage, and no personal data —
every fixture is a generated tone or test
pattern, and the only "metadata" written
anywhere is the lab's own invented title,
artist, and album. Nothing in the guide
required the network.
